What is GIDF

Indian dance is not one thing.

It is Kalbeliya from the desert of Rajasthan. Chhau from the tribal heartlands of Odisha. Lavani from Maharashtra. Kuthu from Tamil Nadu. Giddha from Punjab — each with its own history, its own community, its own way of moving through the world.

Every May, GIDF brings these traditions to Ghent — not as performance alone, but as experience. You learn from teachers who carry these forms in their bodies. You dance, you watch, you celebrate with a live band until late.

"Not rushed. Not overproduced. Not reduced to trends."
— Swapnil, December 2025

Curated by Swapnil Dagliya

What to expect

Three days, one building, many ways in.

Every edition is built from the same parts: workshops by day, a gala by night, and a party to open it all.

Opening Party

The night it all begins

A community dance class, live music and a DJ until late. Recent editions have brought a live band and a night of performances to open the weekend.

Workshops

One tradition, one teacher

Master teachers from across India, each teaching their own form hands-on across the weekend — from classical technique to regional folk styles.

Gala Showcase

An evening on stage

A seated theatrical evening at the Shoonya Theatre — faculty, guest artists and the ABC company together, with live music joining the stage in recent editions.

Practical

Good to know.

This is how GIDF always runs. Dates, faculty and prices are confirmed each year.

The venue

Shoonya Dance Centre, Stapelplein 41, 9000 Gent. The whole festival unfolds under one roof — studios, stage and party space.

Who it's for

All levels and backgrounds, ages 12 and up. No prior Indian-dance experience needed — just come ready to move.

Getting there

A 10-minute walk from Gent-Dampoort station. Street parking out front, Dok Noord parking five minutes away, and the city centre ten minutes on foot.

Tickets & refunds

Registration opens ahead of each edition and closes once full. All registrations and tickets are non-transferable and non-refundable.

Questions

The story so far

Four editions, and counting.

Ghent's annual festival of Indian dance, since 2023 — each edition its own mix of workshops, showcases and an opening party.

2026

Edition Four

The year music arrived

Live music joined the festival for the first time — tabla on the workshop floor and a live band at the opening party. A new four-hour deep-dive format joined the weekend, across seven traditions, with a sold-out gala.

7 master teachers — Colleena Shakti · Vaishali Sagar · Shampa Gopikrishna · Hameed Khan Kawa · Manasi Khanna · Desihop · Swapnil Dagliya

2025

Edition Three

The year of joy

The first community class opened the festival up to new people — it stayed on, while the competitions didn't. Shampa, kept away by a visa the year before, finally taught.

6 teachers — Vaishali Sagar · Shampa Gopikrishna · Lavesh Pritmani · Lenka Badriyah · Aakansha Maheshwari · Swapnil Dagliya

2024

Edition Two

Kathakali enters the room

A precision form rarely taught in Belgium joined the line-up, brought by Julien Touati — the year the festival aimed deeper, not just wider. Shampa Gopikrishna was billed for Lavani and Mujra but her visa came in too late; the festival learned its first lesson in international logistics.

7 teachers — Vaishali Sagar · Shreyashee Nag · Aakansha Maheshwari · Navjot Minhas · Jana Jayanti · Julien Touati · Swapnil Dagliya

2023

Edition One

Eight teachers, eight traditions

From Bharatanatyam to BollyHop to Punjab's Bhangra and Bengal's Kathak — the festival's proposition, made on day one: Indian dance in its full breadth, not narrowed to a single school.

8 teachers — Vaishali Sagar · Lavesh Pritmani · Shreyashee Nag · Girish Kumar · RMoves · Jana Jayanti · Dounia Devsena · Swapnil Dagliya
